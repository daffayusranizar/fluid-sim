## Snippet 1 — The integrator itself

```csharp
Vector2 acceleration = particles[i].force / particles[i].density + gravity;

particles[i].velocity += acceleration * dt;      // velocity first
particles[i].position += particles[i].velocity * dt;   // then position using the NEW velocity
```

**Explain:**
- This is **semi-implicit** (symplectic) Euler, not plain explicit Euler. What exactly is the difference, and which single line makes it semi-implicit?
- Why does using the *updated* velocity for the position update help stability? What does it do to energy over time?
- What is a "symplectic" integrator, and why does that matter more for a long simulation than for a short one?
- Where does `dt` come from now, and why is it no longer `Time.deltaTime`?

## Snippet 2 — Deriving the step size

```csharp
float soundSpeed = Mathf.Sqrt(Mathf.Max(0f, stiffness));

float dtCfl = cflFactor * h / (maxSpeed + soundSpeed + 0.0001f);

float dtAcc = maxAccel > 0.0001f
    ? cflFactor * Mathf.Sqrt(h / maxAccel)
    : float.MaxValue;

return Mathf.Min(dtCfl, dtAcc);
```

**Explain:**
- Your equation of state is `p = stiffness · (ρ − ρ₀)`. Show that `c_s = √stiffness`. (Hint: compare it to the Cole form `p = c_s²(ρ − ρ₀)`, so `dp/dρ = c_s²`.)
- State in one sentence what the **CFL condition** physically prevents. What does the letter-stand for, and what does it mean for information to travel "too far" in one step?
- Why is the denominator `(maxSpeed + c_s)` and not just `c_s`? What situation would the extra term catch?
- What does the **acceleration limit** protect against that the CFL limit does not?
- Explain why `stiffness` is **not a free parameter**. What do you buy with high stiffness, and what do you pay?
- If you want a faster simulation, which direction do you move `stiffness`, and what happens to the fluid's appearance?

## Snippet 3 — The frame-time accumulator

```csharp
timeAccumulator += Time.deltaTime;

int steps = 0;

while (timeAccumulator > 0f && steps < maxSubSteps)
{
    float dt = Mathf.Clamp(ComputeStableTimeStep(), minTimeStep, maxTimeStep);
    dt = Mathf.Min(dt, timeAccumulator);

    FindNeighbors();
    ComputeDensity();
    ComputePressure();
    ResetForces();
    ComputePressureForce();
    ComputeViscosityForce();

    Integrate(dt);

    timeAccumulator -= dt;
    steps++;
}

timeAccumulator = 0f;
```

**Explain:**
- Why is `Time.deltaTime` fed into a *buffer* instead of straight into `Integrate()`? What goes wrong on a slow frame if you skip the buffer?
- Why is the **entire solver** inside the loop, rather than computing forces once per frame and only calling `Integrate()` repeatedly? What would be wrong with stale forces?
- What would happen if `dt > timeAccumulator`? Why does `Mathf.Min(dt, timeAccumulator)` matter, and what does it guarantee about simulation time versus real time?
- Explain the **spiral of death**: what happens to the solver across frames if it cannot keep up, and how does that differ from a game's frame rate dropping? Why does discarding the backlog prevent it?
- `maxSubSteps` caps the loop. What happens to the simulation if it is hit repeatedly? Is that a stability problem or just a slowdown?
